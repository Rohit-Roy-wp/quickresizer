# Top 7 Reasons Why Government Exam Portals Reject Uploaded Photos

Every application season, thousands of aspirants miss critical examination deadlines because their photo or signature uploads repeatedly fail or get flagged by review scrutiny. While government recruitment portals often provide vague error messages like *"Invalid image"*, understanding the technical and visual failure triggers will save you hours of stress.

Here are the top seven reasons why portals reject applicant uploads and how to avoid them.

---

## 1. Exceeding the Exact Kilobyte Ceiling

Recruitment servers employ automated byte checkers. If a portal specifies a 50 KB ceiling, an image measuring 51,201 bytes is mathematically 50.001 KB and will fail validation. Always give yourself a 2 KB safety cushion (e.g. aiming for 45–48 KB) to account for portal rounding algorithms.

---

## 2. Facial Distortion Due to Aspect Ratio Stretching

When candidates use basic photo resizers that force new width and height values without aspect ratio preservation, human faces become squished or artificially elongated. Automated facial landmark detection scripts detect unnatural ratios and reject the file. Always use **Crop to Fill** to keep facial geometry intact.

---

## 3. Dark, Busy, or Patterned Backgrounds

Selfies taken in cars, bedrooms, or outdoors with trees in the background are prohibited. Portals demand a clean, monochromatic white, off-white, or light gray background with zero shadows behind the ears.

---

## 4. Glares, Hats, and Head Coverings

Unless worn for religious reasons (in which case the full face from forehead to chin must remain exposed), hats, caps, masks, and headphones are strictly banned. Furthermore, spectacles with anti-reflective coating that create a blue or green flash glare over your pupils will trigger rejection.

---

## 5. Converting PNG with Transparency to JPEG

When candidates save a signature as a transparent PNG and convert it to JPEG using generic converters, the transparent pixels often turn pitch black. A signature enclosed in a black rectangle is instantly rejected. Always composite onto a pure white canvas background.

---

## 6. Heavy Pixelation from Over-Compression

Stepping quality down too far in an attempt to hit a 10 KB target produces noticeable blocky JPEG artifacts. QuickResizer&apos;s binary search algorithm finds the highest possible quality that lands under your target, keeping ink strokes and facial features sharp.

---

## 7. Unsupported File Formats (HEIC / WebP)

While modern smartphones default to Apple HEIC or Google WebP, many legacy government portals only accept standard baseline JPEG (`.jpg`). Always convert your files to standard JPEG before uploading.
